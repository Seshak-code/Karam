# TreeSPICE vs KLU — 2026-10-15 (Roadmap Phase 4; Apple M4 Pro, 14 cores; SuiteSparse 7.12.1)

Each run is one process per solver: `klu_bench <matrix.mtx> <tree|klu> 5`, timing the minimum of 5 repetitions.

- **fs** is factor + solve per Newton iteration, in ms.
  - TreeSPICE keeps its factor memory in a `ContractionCache` and refactors every front. A's sign flips between repetitions: the pivots stay the same and the values change. This is what the engine's Newton loop pays.
  - KLU: `klu_refactor` + `klu_solve`.
- **GPU** is the GPU build (`-DTREESPICE_GPU=ON`): fronts of dimension >= 512 are factored in FP32 on the GPU (Metal via Dawn), followed by FP64 refinement.
- **flops** are the factorization flops.
- **omega** is the componentwise backward error after the engine's 2 refinement steps.

## What closed the gap

The 2026-10-13 baseline was 3-2200x slower than KLU, and 9 of these 17 matrices did not finish in 300 s. Every matrix now finishes; the slowest, G2_circuit, takes 1.3 s on the CPU.

- **The ordering.** BFS-level nested dissection builds huge dense separators on small-world circuit graphs, up to 20 000x KLU's flops.
  - The planner now also runs an approximate minimum degree on the quotient graph, as KLU's AMD does.
  - It forms fundamental supernodes, then CHOLMOD-rule relaxed amalgamation.
  - It keeps whichever plan costs less, at flops + 1e4 per front. A front's fixed cost is about 0.8 us against about 1e-10 s per flop.
  - Grids still choose nested dissection; transistor-level circuits choose minimum degree.
- **A persistent worker pool** instead of spawning threads per tree level.
- **Remaining gap (1.1-3x on the small matrices):** the fixed cost per front.
  - KLU's left-looking sparse LU does no dense front assembly.
  - TreeSPICE does up to 18x KLU's flops on the sparsest matrices, but within 2x on the large ones, where its dense fronts and threads win.

## The GPU

- **Dense FP32 LU of large fronts (`math/gpu_front_lu.cpp`):**
  - It pays off where fronts stay large after ordering: G2_circuit 3.3x faster than the CPU path (7.2x faster than KLU), ASIC_100k 2.1x, the grid 2.5x.
  - The cutoff was moved from 256 to 512. At 256 the medium fronts lost (rajat15 46 ms against 30 ms on the CPU); 512 is best on every matrix tried (256, 384, 512 and 768).
  - No solve needed the FP64 redo.
- **Whole tree on the GPU, tried and deleted.** This was one command buffer, one batched dispatch per level, superposition as a gather, and static pivots. It was correct: backward error 3e-16 to 7e-15 after 2-8 FP32 refinement round trips, except on scircuit, whose refinement stalled at 2e-7 and fell back to the CPU. But it was 1.5-10x slower than the CPU on every matrix:
  - circuit_2: 6.3 ms against 0.59 ms;
  - bcircuit: 36 ms against 8.0 ms;
  - ASIC_100k: 0.69 s against 0.25 s.
- **Why the tree lost:**
  - A front's elimination is a sequence of steps. On the GPU each step is a workgroup reduction plus barriers, about a dozen barriers per pivot.
  - After the ordering, circuit fronts are small (dimensions of 20-60), so this latency, not arithmetic, sets the time. A 1000-wide front on one workgroup leaves the rest of the GPU idle.
  - FP32 factors also cost 2-8 extra GPU round trips of refinement.
  - A GPU tree would need blocked multi-workgroup kernels for the large fronts and subgroup-level ones for the small. Neither fixes the per-step latency, and the CPU path is already at KLU's level.
